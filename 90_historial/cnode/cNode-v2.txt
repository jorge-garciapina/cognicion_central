1.1 Definition
A Cognitive Node (cNode) is an ontology packaged as an integrable unit in a cognitive graph. (SERÍA BUENO PONER cognitive graph EN LA SECCIÓN 3) KEY TERMS )
It is a deliberate projection of a cognitive process (an idea, concept, design, plan, or method) (DEBIDO QUE BUSCO MUCHA PRECISIÓN, SERÍA BUENO DEFINIR cognitive process EN 3) KEY TERMS )
into an explicit structure that preserves identity under adaptation (¿ES ESTO LO ÚNICO QUE HACE?). A cNode makes meaning
operable by specifying:
(QUIERO QUE EXPANDAS UN POCO MÁS LA EXPLICACIÓN, QUIZÁS NO SÓLO ENTRE PARÉNTESIS, SINO COMO ALGO QUE APORTE UNA IDEA MÁS PROFUNDA, QUIZÁS UN EJEMPLO)
(a) a controlled vocabulary of entities and relations, (SERÍA BUENO QUE INDICARAS CÓMO ES QUE CONTROLA EL VOCABULARIO)
(b) invariants (what cannot change without breaking identity),
(c) a domain of variation (what may change),
(d) procedures (how the ontology is used or derived), (AQUÍ HAY ALGO QUE LLAMA LA ATENCIÓN: ESTÁS USANDO "ONTOLOGÍA" PERO AÚN NO SE DEFINE, YO SÉ QUE MÁS ABAJO LO HACES, PERO LA PREGUNTA ES ¿ES CORRECTO HACER ESTO? SIENTO QUE PODRÍA CAUSAR CONFUSIÓN, ES POR ESO QUE PUSE LA NOTA AL INICIO, EN LA QUE PIDO QUE AÑADAS UNA IDEA, QUIZÁS UN EJEMPLO EN VEZ DE LO QUE PONES ENTRE PARÉNTESIS)
(e) validation criteria (how to test compatibility and correctness). ¿COMPATIBILIDAD Y correctness DE QUÉ? DE NUEVO, AQUÍ SE RESALTA LA IMPORTANCIA DEL COMENTARIO EN QUE PIDO EXPANDIR LAS IDEAS EN LOS PARÉNTESIS
A cNode is designed to be compositional: it can contain internal nodes (MÁS ABAJO, EN LA SECCIÓN 5.1 Component: cognitive_node MUESTRO COMO EL TÉRMINO "NODO" PUEDE CAUSAR CONFUSIÓN, LA FORMA EN LA QUE LO USAS AQUÍ ES EL EJEMPLO; ESTA ES LA IMPORTANCIA DE DEFINIR CORRECTAMENTE LOS TÉRMINOS EN LA SECCIÓN 3) KEY TERMS, NO ES ALGO COSMÉTICO, SINO ALGO VITAL- NO DIGO QUE NO USES LA PALABRA "NODE" SÓLO QUIERO EVITAR LA CONFUSIÓN ENTRE CÓMO SE USA) 
that are either plain concepts or other cNodes (ESTO ES VERDAD, SE ACERCA A LO QUE PIENSO), enabling construction of complex systems
as networks of ontologies.

2) WHY THIS CONCEPT EXISTS (THE PROBLEM IT SOLVES)

2.1 Problem statement
Humans frequently communicate concepts using only natural language, which produces (NO NECESARIAMENTE, NO EN TODOS LOS CASOS, SIENTO QUE AQUÍ ESTÁS AFIRMANDO COMO SI SIEMPRE SUCEDIERA)
ambiguity: different readers ("PUEDEN", "MAY", "MIGHT") project different internal instances from the same word (for example,
one person hears "dog" and imagines a childhood Dalmatian, another imagines a German Shepherd).
Both are "correct" in ordinary language, but this divergence breaks consistency when the goal is
to design, validate, or integrate systems of ideas (SIENTO QUE LO QUE HABLAS AL INICIO NO ES SUFICIENTEMENTE FUERTE PARA AFIRMAR 
QUE "this divergence breaks consistency when the goal is to design, validate, or integrate systems of ideas", HACE FALTA UNA 
NECESIDAD MÁS FUERTE, USA LOS COMENTARIOS QUE TE HAGO EN ESTA REVISIÓN, O LO QUE HEMOS DISCUTIDO EN EL CHAT PARA HALLAR ALGO MEJOR). 
The problem becomes more severe when multiple projects, multiple time periods, or multiple stakeholders must share and evolve the
same concept.

2.2 Structural constraint that makes the problem persistent
The constraint is structural: human cognition compresses complexity by implicit context (DEFINE implicit context EN SECCIÓN 3 ) and
personal exemplars, while large systems require stable identity (EXPLICA stable identity, PUEDE CAUSAR AMBIGUEDAD)
and explicit integration rules.

  - implicit context ES ALGO HUMANO, Y ES "OPUESTO" A stable identity LO CUAL ES DE LA IA... NO VEO EN QUÉ SON DISTINTOS, POR ESO TE PIDO DEFINIRLOS
  - personal exemplars ES ALGO HUMANO, Y ES "OPUESTO" explicit integration rules... MISMA NOTA ANTERIOR.

In addition, Artificial Intelligence (AI) systems can ingest large text, but if the text lacks
explicit structure (invariants, allowed variation, validation rules), the AI and the human can

3.1 Ontology (as used here)
An ontology is a structured specification of a domain that defines entities, relations, and the
constraints that preserve identity and enable consistent reasoning. In this framework:
Cognitive Node = Ontology. (EN VEZ DE DAR UNA IGUALDAD QUE TIENE QUE ACEPTARSE, PREFERIRÍA QUE DIERAS UNA COMPARACIÓN O UNA DESCRIPCIÓN 
EN LA QUE MUESTRES QUE SON IGUALES.)

3.2 Weak ontology versus strong ontology
- Weak ontology: minimal but explicit identity boundary and invariants; vocabulary and rules may
  be partially open; validation is present but may be lightweight.
- Strong ontology: highly specified vocabulary, relations, constraints, and validation; the space
  of acceptable interpretations is tightly controlled.
Both are ontologies, and both are valid cNodes; they differ by degree of closure, not by kind. 
(NO ME QUEDA CLARO LO QUE DICES, AÑADE EJEMPLOS EN LOS QUE MUESTRES QUÉ ES UNA ONTOLOGÍA DEBIL, UNA MEDIA Y UNA FUERTE, 
PUEDE SER EL MISMO EJEMPLO, SIMPLEMENTE AÑADE MÁS DETALLES LO CUAL PERMITA VER, PODRÍAS USAR EL EJEMPLO DE LOS PERROS)

3.3 Projection (as used here)
Projection is the operation that maps an internal cognitive process to an external, explicit
structure. The result of the projection is the cNode itself. Projection is not a "section" in
the document; it is the generating act of the ontology package.
(PERO LA PROYECCIÓN DEL mNode -VER NOTA MÁS ARRIBA- NO PROVIEDE DE CUALQUIER external, explicit
structur, SINO DE LA MENTE DE UN HUMANO. ES IMPORTANTE CLARIFICAR SIEMPRE.  )

3.4 Identity boundary
The identity boundary is the minimal set of properties that must remain invariant for the cNode
to remain the same cNode across adaptations. If these properties are violated, the cNode becomes
a different ontology.
(POR EJEMPLO...)

3.5 Invariant
An invariant is not merely a statement that should remain true. An invariant is a condition that
must be preserved across the entire allowed domain of variation; violating it breaks identity.

3.6 Domain of variation
The domain of variation is the explicitly permitted space of adaptations that can occur without
breaking identity (for example, adding examples, extending non-essential terms, or refining
validation), as long as invariants remain preserved.
SOBRE ESTE TÉRMINO EN PARTICULAR QUIERO QUE PONGAS ATENCIÓN EN LOS DOCUMENTOS QUE TE ENVÍO (LOS DOCUMENTOS NO SON SÓLO PARA 
ESTE TÉRMINO, PERO TE AYUDARÁN A ENTENDER)

3.7 Orchestrator
The orchestrator is the component that specifies internal composition rules for the cNode:
how internal sub-nodes interact, in what order procedures run, what the export surface is, and
how integration constraints are enforced. It is a role inside the cNode package, not a separate
"document type" that replaces the term orchestrator.

3.8 Technical metaphor (functional, not decorative)
A technical metaphor is a controlled mapping layer that transfers structure between domains.
It is used only when it increases precision and interoperability. It is not used as a substitute
for invariants or formal identity.

3.9 Always-integrable (design intent)
Always-integrable means: every cNode must expose an explicit integration surface that allows it
to connect to other cNodes deterministically. Integration may require negotiation (aliases,
adapters), but it must not require hidden context.

4) MINIMAL STRUCTURE / INVARIANTS
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
This section defines the smallest structure that still qualifies as a cNode (that is, as an
ontology package intended for integration).

4.1 Minimal structure (conceptual)
A minimal cNode must contain:
(1) Identity boundary: explicit invariants + explicit domain of variation.
(2) Ontology core: explicit entities and relations (a minimal vocabulary + relation set).
(3) Orchestrator: explicit rules for internal composition and export.
(4) Integration surface: explicit description of how the cNode connects to the external graph.

4.2 Identity-defining invariants (must hold in all valid instances)
Invariant I1 — Ontology identity is explicit
The cNode must explicitly define what entities and relations exist in its domain.
If you remove explicit entities/relations, you remove the ontology, therefore you remove the cNode.

Invariant I2 — Invariants and allowed variation are explicit
The cNode must separate "what must not change" from "what may change".
If you cannot distinguish essential identity from optional detail, the cNode cannot be validated.
ESTOY DE ACUERDO CON ESO. EN LOS EJEMPLOS QUE ADJUNTO ES POSIBLE VER EJEMPLOS DE ESTO

Invariant I3 — Operationality exists (procedures and/or validation)
The cNode must include operational hooks: how to use it, derive from it, or test it.
If it is only descriptive prose, it is not an operational ontology package.

Invariant I4 — Integration is explicit
The cNode must declare how it integrates into a network of cNodes (export surface and constraints).
If integration rules are implicit, cross-project reuse will drift.

Invariant I5 — Compositionality is permitted
The cNode must allow internal nodes to be either concepts or other cNodes.
If internal composition is forbidden, scaling to complex systems becomes brittle.

4.3 If you remove X, it is no longer a cNode
- Remove explicit invariants and allowed variation → not a cNode (identity is not preservable).
- Remove explicit entities/relations → not a cNode (no ontology remains).
- Remove explicit integration surface → not a cNode (cannot reliably join a network of cNodes).

5.2 Component: graph_patch (integration surface)
Role: Specifies how the cNode integrates into an external graph of cNodes.
Typical contents:
- export_surface:
  - PROVIDES: capabilities offered to the graph (what this ontology enables)
  - REQUIRES: capabilities required from the graph (dependencies)
  - CONSTRAINTS: invariants the graph must respect when integrating this cNode
  - TYPES: canonical types used at the interface
  - ALIASES: mapping of external terms to internal vocabulary (synonym control)
- integration_profile:
  - provides/requires versioning
  - adapters (edge aliases, type aliases, data mappers)
  - negotiation steps (discover → check → alias-map → validate → adapt → merge → report)
  - merge policy (deterministic conflict rules and provenance)
graph_patch is not "optional decoration"; it is the explicit form of always-integrable design.

5.3 Component: orchestrator (internal composition controller)
Role: Defines internal interaction among sub-nodes and the cNode export interface.
Typical contents:
- internal node registry (what sub-nodes exist)
- internal edge rules (how sub-nodes connect)
- execution order for procedures (if any)
- interface assembly (how export_surface is computed from internals)
- consistency enforcement (guards that preserve invariants during updates)

5.4 Component: internal_nodes (subunits)
Role: The building blocks inside a cNode.
Internal nodes can be:
- concept nodes: minimal conceptual units (typed entities, relations, constraints)
- nested cNodes: full ontologies embedded inside this cNode (recursion)
This is the mechanism that enables complex concepts to be assembled as networks.

5.5 Component: technical_metaphor_layer (functional mapping layer)
Role: Transfers structure when formalism is incomplete or when bridging domains.
This layer MUST be controlled to prevent ambiguity:
- metaphor_term: "port", "mold", "scaffold" (examples)
- mapped_structure: what ontology structure it corresponds to (entities/relations/procedures)
- constraints: what the metaphor is NOT allowed to imply
- evidence: why the mapping is valid in this context
This layer is used to accelerate understanding without replacing invariants.

7.1 Canonical example (concept-level): "DOG" cNode
Core idea:
Two humans can both correctly think "dog" while imagining different instances.
A DOG cNode prevents divergence by encoding identity and allowed variation.

DOG.cognitive_node (weak ontology example):
- entities: Dog, Human
- relations: (Dog hasTrait Trait), (Human owns Dog), (Dog isAnimal Animal)
- invariants (identity boundary): "Dog is a domesticated animal capable of the role X"
  (the exact invariant must be chosen by the project; the point is: it is explicit)
- variation_domain: breed, color, size, personal exemplar, cultural context
- validation: checks that an instance respects invariants while allowing variation

DOG.graph_patch:
- PROVIDES: "pet-concept", "domesticated-animal-role"
- REQUIRES: "animal-base-ontology" (optional)
- ALIASES: maps "canine" → Dog, etc.

Result:
Dalmatian and German Shepherd become valid variations under the same identity boundary,
instead of uncontrolled divergent meanings.

7.2 Edge example (stress test): "DOG" that violates invariants
If an instance labeled "dog" violates the identity invariants (for example, it is not an animal),
then it is no longer a valid instance of the DOG cNode. This clarifies boundaries.

7.3 System-level example: building a complex concept from cNodes
Suppose a project defines:
- cNode:Scheduling
- cNode:TimeZone
- cNode:Identity
A larger cNode:ClinicSystem can contain those cNodes as internal_nodes, and orchestrator rules
define how they interact. graph_patch then exposes the composite ontology to the larger graph.

9) WORKFLOW LOOP (HOW IT IS USED ITERATIVELY)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────

EN LA SIGUIENTE PREGUNTA TE PEDIRÉ QUE TRABAJEMOS CREANDO UN EJEMPLO QUE LUEGO PONDREMOS AQUÍ PARA MEJORAR LA DEFINICIÓN. 
A repeatable cNode workflow is:

Step 1 — Capture the internal cognitive process
- Identify the concept to externalize (idea, plan, method, design).
- Write the minimal problem statement (why it must exist).

Step 2 — Project to explicit identity
- Declare entities and relations.
- Declare invariants (identity boundary) and domain of variation.

Step 3 — Build operational hooks
- Specify procedures and validation criteria.
- Add technical metaphors only if they map to explicit structure.

Step 4 — Assemble the package
- Fill cognitive_node (internal ontology).
- Fill orchestrator (internal composition rules).
- Fill graph_patch (export surface and integration profile).

Step 5 — Integrate into the graph
- Negotiate aliases and adapters with existing cNodes.
- Merge deterministically with provenance.

Step 6 — Iterate without breaking identity
- Expand examples, strengthen validation, refine vocabulary.
- Only change invariants deliberately, because that changes identity.

Step 7 — Version the cNode
- Use Semantic Versioning (SemVer): MAJOR.MINOR.PATCH
  - MAJOR: identity boundary changes (invariants change)
  - MINOR: compatible extensions inside allowed variation
  - PATCH: clarifications, examples, non-identity notes

10) PRACTICAL NOTES (OPTIONAL, NOT IDENTITY-DEFINING)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
- Vocabulary control is a first-class requirement. If synonyms are allowed, they must be mapped.
- Technical metaphors must never replace invariants. They only accelerate understanding when
  anchored to explicit structure and constraints.
- Always-integrable does not mean "no conflicts ever". It means conflicts are representable,
  negotiable, and resolvable without hidden context.
- Avoid term collisions: do not reuse reserved terms (for example, "artifact") inside the cNode
  definition unless explicitly redefining them with strong disambiguation.

11) QUICK SELF-CHECKLIST (UNIT TEST OF THE DEFINITION)
───────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
Use this checklist to validate whether a candidate object qualifies as a cNode:

[ ] Does it explicitly define entities and relations (ontology core), not only prose?
[ ] Does it explicitly separate invariants (identity boundary) from allowed variation?
[ ] Does it specify procedures and/or validation criteria (operationality)?
[ ] Does it include an orchestrator role that defines internal composition and export assembly?
[ ] Does it include an explicit integration surface (graph_patch) for joining a cNode network?
[ ] Can internal nodes be concepts or other cNodes (compositional recursion)?
[ ] Are ambiguous terms disambiguated and synonyms controlled (aliases mapped)?
[ ] Can a reader with zero prior context reuse this definition without hidden assumptions?
[ ] If the cNode changes, is versioning consistent with identity changes versus extensions?
